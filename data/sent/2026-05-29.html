Internal Blocks Meet External Tactics: A Losing Game

9 picks · AI & Marketing first · Read online →

This week leaned hard into the inner game — wounds, nervous systems, identity loops, and the uncomfortable truth that most "hacks" are just avoidance in a trench coat. Marketing and psychology kept blurring into each other, which honestly tracks.

Marketing

The Triangle of Yapping Framework

A three-part structure for talking-head videos that actually hold attention.

  • Open with T/T (Thought or Topic) — drop viewers mid-conversation, skip the intro fluff.
  • Layer in MAES (Metaphor, Analogy, Example, Story) to make dry topics land.
  • Close with A/N (Application or Next Steps) so viewers know what to do next.
  • Stacked together, it gives every video a repeatable spine for retention.

Content Hacks Are Fake — Learn Fundamentals Instead

A blunt reminder that the "secret trick" economy is mostly fiction.

  • VPN posting, copying links to Google, tagging big creators — fabricated, none of it moves the needle.
  • There's no magic posting time, hashtag formula, or algorithm cheat code.
  • We chase hacks because certainty feels safer than building real skill.
  • Growth comes from knowing what to post, how to position, and how to optimize each video.

$4K/Day Using AI to Find Demand Gaps

Mine competitor complaints, find the gap, ship the product.

  • "Complaint harvesting": prompt AI to pull negative reviews from top sellers in your niche.
  • Look for the hidden demand gap — the recurring pain point no current product solves.
  • Use Canva AI to spin up a digital product targeting that exact missing solution.
  • Products built on unmet demand sell themselves because they're the only real answer.

Your Unhealed Wounds Are Sabotaging Your Marketing

Eight self-sabotage patterns in coaches and healers, each tied to a specific wound.

  • Fear of selling = past manipulative sales experiences; fix by healing the money story and clarifying your transformation.
  • Inconsistency = fear of success and distrust of future self; fix by committing to your "Main Character Message" before you feel ready.
  • Perfectionism = protection tied to conditional love; fix by posting imperfectly for one person, one problem.
  • Metric obsession = outsourcing self-worth to an algorithm; fix by anchoring to WHY and showing up in service, not audition.

Psychology

Why Your Body Rejects Big Money

Unfamiliar wealth reads as threat — and your identity quietly pushes it back out.

  • The cringe of imagining $30k/month is a nervous system safety response, not a character flaw.
  • When money arrives, an old identity loop activates and finds a way to spend or repel it.
  • That same loop (say, self-trust) shows up across relationships and work, not just finances.
  • Breaking it means naming the loop, then upgrading the identity that has to hold the money.

by @Kay · Watch →

Retrain Your Brain to Stop Obsessing Over Someone

A 30-second morning practice aimed at the amygdala's abandonment alarm.

  • The amygdala flags abandonment as danger (wired ages 0–6), triggering chemicals that force repetitive thoughts.
  • The brain is most malleable in alpha/theta/delta states — right at waking, falling asleep, or in deep meditation.
  • The moment you wake, spend 30 seconds speaking to yourself with extreme tenderness, like to a baby.
  • After ~30 days, obsessive thoughts thin out — hours apart, then days, then weeks.

Find Your Hunger or Stay Broke

A hard-edged take that hunger, not talent or luck, is the real variable.

  • Talent and luck are overrated; sustained hunger and sacrifice do the heavy lifting.
  • While others rest or celebrate, high achievers are still hunting the next win.
  • The price is real: strained relationships, missed moments, social isolation.
  • Ask what comfort you'll sacrifice that others won't — no answer means no edge.
