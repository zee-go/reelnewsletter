The 1940s Japanese Method That Cured Depression

A silence-based practice from wartime Japan that reached remission where modern treatments stalled.

  • Japanese psychologists developed a seven-day silent practice in the 1940s that produced complete remission in treatment-resistant depression.
  • The method works by observing and allowing inner experience — not suppressing, fixing, or escaping it.
  • Much of our suffering comes from the stories we attach to other people's behavior, not the behavior itself.
  • Real freedom starts when you change your relationship to your inner experience, not to the other person.
